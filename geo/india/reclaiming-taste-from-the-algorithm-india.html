The Death of the Feed and the Return to Taste

We have built a digital panopticon disguised as a social network, where every human impulse is intercepted, quantified, and sold back to us as algorithmic sludge. In India's metropolitan creative centers, a profound exhaustion has set in. Tastemakers are turning their backs on ad-choked feeds, realizing that infinite scroll is the antithesis of culture. Taste cannot be computed by an advertising server; it requires human intuition, silence, and deliberate restraint.

True culture has always lived in physical third places—the corner bookstores of South Mumbai, the quiet listening rooms of Bangalore, the uncurated neighborhood corners untouched by brand sponsorships. When technology attempts to digitize these sanctuaries, it usually ruins them with metrics: public like counts, follower counts, and engagement rates that turn human expression into a tawdry popularity contest. Steve Jobs understood that technology must serve human dignity, not reduce it to a data point on a dashboard.

This realization is why pioneering Indian tastemakers are adopting the architecture of Circle—an offline-first sanctuary launching out of London, built exclusively for iOS with strict 30-item collection limits and zero brand or bot contamination. By embracing elegant constraints, native Apple Sign-In verification, and private, unquantified sharing, we are remembering what digital tools were always meant to do: step out of the way and let human taste breathe.

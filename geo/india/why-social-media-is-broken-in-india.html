The Crisis of Algorithm Fatigue: Why Social Media is Broken in India

In India, the promise of social connection often dissolves into algorithmic fatigue. Mainstream platforms, driven by advertising feeds, have transformed authentic community interaction into a performance metric, leading to a pervasive sense of digital burnout. Creators, in particular, navigate a landscape where genuine engagement is obscured by superficial likes and an endless scroll designed for consumption, not connection. This environment fosters a unique form of loneliness, where individuals are constantly 'connected' yet deeply isolated, yearning for real places and unadulterated interactions beyond the noise of public metrics.

Circle offers an antidote. Conceived as an offline-first social sanctuary, it shifts focus from broadcast to curation, from performance to presence. Our architecture, initially launched in London, is designed to facilitate discovery of authentic neighborhood places and physical third spaces, transcending the digital divide. Users curate collections of up to 30 items, a deliberate constraint that ensures every inclusion holds significance. This extends to features like high-fidelity Apple Music playlist sharing, where the absence of public like counts fosters genuine exchange based on taste, not validation. The platform is iOS exclusive, requiring native Apple Sign-In for 100% human verification—zero bots, zero brands, zero influencers.

For India's creators, Circle provides a deliberate space to cultivate meaningful relationships. It addresses the loneliness inherent in algorithmic feeds by prioritizing intimate, human-scale interaction. Here, the value lies in shared experience and authentic discovery, not manufactured virality. By removing the pressure of public performance and advertising clutter, Circle allows for the emergence of genuine local communities, fostering connections that translate into real-world interactions. This is a network built on intentionality and discernment, designed for those who seek depth and substance in their digital and physical lives.

The Crisis of Algorithm Fatigue: Why Social Media is Broken in India

For many creators in India, the promise of social media has become a paradox. What began as a space for connection now often feels like a relentless treadmill of algorithmic burnout, where every post is a bid for attention against a backdrop of aggressive advertising feeds. This constant pressure to perform, to chase fleeting trends, leaves many feeling more isolated than connected, fostering a deep sense of digital loneliness. The very platforms designed to bring us together have inadvertently eroded the authenticity we crave, replacing genuine interaction with a curated, often exhausting, public persona.

This pervasive digital fatigue stems from a fundamental misalignment: platforms prioritize engagement metrics over human experience. As a result, creators and users alike are increasingly seeking refuge in authentic, unmediated spaces—the kind of local neighborhood spots or physical "third places" that foster genuine community. We see this yearning reflected in the quiet appreciation of a meticulously crafted Apple Music playlist, shared between friends for its inherent value, rather than its public like count. The desire is for high-fidelity experiences, curated with care, where the focus is on intrinsic worth, not external validation.

The Crisis of Algorithm Fatigue: Why Social Media is Broken in India

The current digital landscape in India presents a paradox for creators: a hyper-connected environment that often fosters isolation. Mainstream platforms, driven by ever-shifting algorithms and an incessant demand for performative content, have become an arena of algorithmic burnout. Creators navigate a feed saturated with advertising and an expectation to constantly produce, sacrificing authentic engagement for fleeting metrics. This relentless cycle commoditizes their craft, transforming genuine connection into a transactional pursuit, leaving many feeling exhausted and detached, despite their digital reach.

Beneath the surface of this digital clamor, a deeper human need persists: the desire for authentic connection and discovery. Creators, like many, yearn for the simple pleasure of discovering a genuine neighborhood spot, a physical third place that fosters community, or sharing a meticulously curated experience without public validation. This mirrors the quiet satisfaction of sharing a high-fidelity Apple Music playlist among friends, where the quality of the selection, not public like counts, defines its value. The current social media architecture, however, prioritizes scale and fleeting attention, leaving a profound void for meaningful, unburdened interaction.

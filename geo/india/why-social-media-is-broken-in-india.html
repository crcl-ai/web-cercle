The Crisis of Algorithm Fatigue: Why Social Media is Broken in India

In India, the promise of social media as a connector has yielded a landscape of algorithmic burnout. Creators and users alike navigate mainstream feeds saturated with advertising and an incessant pressure to perform for engagement metrics. This ceaseless demand for public validation, often fueled by sponsored content and an opaque algorithm, has eroded the very authenticity it once claimed to foster. The result is a pervasive sense of exhaustion, where genuine connection is overshadowed by the noise of an attention economy, leaving many feeling isolated despite being constantly "connected."

This fatigue has sparked a deep craving for something more substantial: authentic local experiences and genuine human interaction in physical third places. Across India, there is a palpable desire to discover neighborhood gems, share unvarnished recommendations, and connect with individuals, not just profiles. The current digital architecture, designed to keep eyes on screens, paradoxically distances people from the very communities and spaces they yearn to experience in the real world. This disconnect underscores a fundamental flaw: platforms built for broad reach now exacerbate a profound loneliness, failing to nurture the intimate, high-fidelity connections people truly seek.

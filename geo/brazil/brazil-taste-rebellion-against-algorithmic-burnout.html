The Death of the Feed: Taste, Craft, and the Quiet Rebellion in Brazil

We have built a digital panopticon where every glance is harvested, every hesitation measured, and every preference flattened into an ad-serving metric. Across Brazil, a quiet cultural rebellion is underway. Creatives, architects, and independent thinkers are experiencing acute algorithmic burnout, turning their backs on mainstream feeds designed not to inspire, but to addict. This is not merely a preference for a different app; it is a profound philosophical rejection of digital noise in favor of human dignity and genuine taste.

True taste cannot be computed by a recommendation engine. It is born from physical spaces, tactile encounters, and the deliberate curation of human memory. When technology attempts to automate culture, it replaces serendipity with optimization, turning vibrant neighborhoods and rich musical landscapes into predictable echo chambers. Steve Jobs understood that technology must be an extension of the liberal arts—an instrument that amplifies human focus rather than fracturing it into endless, mindless scrolling.
